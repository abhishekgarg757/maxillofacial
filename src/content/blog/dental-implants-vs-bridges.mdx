When you lose a tooth, two of the most common replacement options are a **dental
implant** and a **fixed bridge**. Both can look natural and restore your bite —
but they work in different ways. Here is a clear, balanced comparison.

## How each option works

A **dental implant** is a small titanium post placed into the jawbone to act as
an artificial tooth root. After it fuses with the bone, a crown is attached on
top — replacing the tooth without involving the neighbouring teeth.

A **bridge** replaces a missing tooth by anchoring a false tooth to the teeth on
either side, which are shaped to hold crowns that support the bridge.

## Key differences to weigh up

- **Neighbouring teeth:** An implant stands alone. A traditional bridge requires
  reshaping the adjacent teeth, even if they are healthy.
- **Bone health:** An implant stimulates the jawbone much like a natural root,
  helping to preserve bone. A bridge does not.
- **Longevity:** Implants are designed to last many years; dental studies
  commonly report high long-term success. Bridges also last well but may need
  replacing sooner.
- **Time:** A bridge can often be completed in a few weeks. An implant takes
  longer because the bone needs months to fuse with the post.
- **Suitability:** Implants need enough healthy bone (grafting can help when it
  is lacking). Bridges can be a good choice when neighbouring teeth already need
  crowns.

## So which is better?

There is no single right answer — it depends on the health of your mouth, the
amount of bone you have, your timeline and your priorities. Many people prefer
implants for their independence and bone-preserving benefits, while a bridge can
be an excellent, faster solution in the right situation.

The best next step is a personal assessment, where your options can be explained
clearly and matched to your needs.
