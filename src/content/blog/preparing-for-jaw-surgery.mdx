Corrective jaw surgery can feel like a big step. Understanding the journey ahead
is one of the best ways to feel calm and in control. Here is a clear overview of
what usually happens — though remember every plan is personalised in
consultation.

## Before surgery: orthodontics and planning

For most people, jaw surgery is part of a combined plan with an orthodontist.
Braces or clear aligners first align the teeth within each jaw so that, once the
jaws are repositioned, the bite fits together correctly.

Your surgeon will also take detailed records — often including a CT scan and
digital models — to plan the exact movements in 3D. This lets the whole team
rehearse the surgery and prepare custom guides for a precise, predictable
result.

## The days before

- Follow any instructions about eating and drinking before your anaesthetic.
- Arrange time off — many people plan around two weeks away from work or study.
- Stock up on soft, nourishing foods such as soups, smoothies, yoghurt and dal.
- Prepare a comfortable place to rest with pillows to keep your head elevated.

## During surgery

Jaw surgery is performed under general anaesthesia. In the great majority of
cases, the incisions are made **inside the mouth**, so there are no visible
scars on the face. Small titanium plates and screws hold the bone securely while
it heals.

## Recovery: what to expect week by week

Swelling is greatest in the first few days and then steadily settles. A soft or
liquid diet protects the healing bone, and gentle oral hygiene keeps things
clean and comfortable.

- **Week 1–2:** Most swelling settles; many people return to light routine.
- **Weeks 6–12:** Bone heals; diet is gradually advanced under guidance.
- **Several months:** Final settling of the bite and completion of orthodontics.

## Looking after yourself

Good nutrition, rest and gentle movement all help healing. Keep your follow-up
appointments, and don't hesitate to call the clinic if anything worries you.
