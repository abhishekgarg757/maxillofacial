The temporomandibular joint (TMJ) is the hinge that connects your lower jaw to
your skull. When it or the surrounding muscles aren't working comfortably, it's
called a **temporomandibular disorder (TMD)** — and it's surprisingly common.

## Common signs

- Jaw pain or tenderness, especially when chewing
- Clicking, popping or grating sounds when you open or close
- A feeling that the jaw is stiff, tight or occasionally "locks"
- Headaches or aching around the ears, cheeks or temples

## The good news: most cases improve without surgery

The majority of TMJ problems settle with simple, conservative measures. These
are almost always the first approach:

- **Resting the jaw** and avoiding very hard or chewy foods for a while
- **A bite splint or night guard**, particularly if you clench or grind
- **Jaw physiotherapy and gentle exercises**
- **Anti-inflammatory or muscle-relaxant medication** for short periods
- **Managing stress and habits** that load the jaw

## When to seek specialist help

Consider seeing an oral & maxillofacial surgeon if:

- The pain is persistent or worsening despite conservative care
- You have significant difficulty opening your mouth, or the jaw locks
- There has been an injury to the jaw or joint
- Imaging suggests a structural problem inside the joint

## What specialist treatment can offer

When needed, treatment follows a **step-wise approach** — always favouring the
least invasive option that will solve the problem. This ranges from minimally
invasive techniques such as **arthrocentesis** (gently washing out the joint)
through to keyhole **arthroscopy**, and, for advanced cases, open joint surgery
or reconstruction.

The key message is reassuring: most people never need surgery, and for those who
do, modern, targeted options can make a real difference.
