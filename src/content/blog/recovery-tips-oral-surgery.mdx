Recovering from oral surgery — whether it’s a simple extraction, dental implant placement, or corrective jaw surgery — requires patience and attention to aftercare. While every procedure is different, these evidence-informed tips can help support your healing.

## Immediate post-operative care (first 48 hours)

### Protect the clot

After any surgery where an incision is made, a blood clot forms in the socket. This clot is the foundation of healing — if it dislodges, you can develop a painful condition called dry socket.

- **Bite gently** on the gauze provided, changing it when it becomes saturated
- **Do not rinse, spit, or use straws** for the first 24 hours — the suction motion can dislodge the clot
- **Avoid smoking** for at least 72 hours (nicotine constricts blood vessels and significantly increases dry socket risk)
- **Sleep with your head elevated** on 2–3 pillows to reduce swelling

### Manage swelling and discomfort

- **Ice packs** applied to the cheek in 15-minute intervals (on/off) are most effective within the first 24 hours
- **After 48 hours**, switch to **warm compresses** to help resolve existing swelling
- **Take prescribed pain medication** proactively — waiting until pain becomes severe makes it harder to manage
- **Anti-inflammatory medication** (such as ibuprofen) can reduce both pain and swelling when cleared by your surgeon

## Nutrition during recovery

### First 3–5 days: Liquid to very soft diet

- **Broths and soups** (warm, not hot)
- **Smoothies and protein shakes** — avoid using straws; pour into a cup or use a spoon
- **Yogurt, pudding, and applesauce**
- **Mashed potatoes, avocado, and hummus**
- **Oatmeal, cream of wheat, and soft cereals** (well-cooked and smooth)

### Days 5–10: Gradually add soft solids

- **Scrambled eggs or tofu**
- **Well-cooked pasta, rice, or grains**
- **Soft fruits** like bananas, melon, or peaches (without skin if difficult to chew)
- **Steamed vegetables** without tough skins or seeds
- **Fish that flakes easily**, such as salmon or cod

### Foods to avoid

- **Crunchy or hard items**: chips, nuts, seeds, hard candies
- **Chewy or sticky foods**: caramel, gummy candies, tough bread
- **Spicy or acidic items** that can irritate the surgical site
- **Very hot foods and drinks** for the first 24–48 hours
- **Alcohol** — it can interact with pain medication and impair healing

## Oral hygiene during recovery

- **First 24 hours**: Do not rinse your mouth. Gentle brushing is okay, avoiding the surgical site.
- **Day 2 onward**: Begin gentle saltwater rinses (½ teaspoon salt in 8 oz warm water) after meals and before bed. Swish gently and let the solution flow out — do not spit forcefully.
- **Resume regular brushing** after the first few days, carefully avoiding the immediate surgical area.
- **Your surgeon may prescribe** an antimicrobial rinse (such as chlorhexidine) — use as directed.

## Activity guidelines

### First 72 hours

- **Rest extensively** — plan to take at least 2–3 days off work or school
- **Avoid strenuous exercise**, heavy lifting, or vigorous activity that increases blood flow to the head
- **Avoid bending over** for extended periods, especially in the first week
- **No smoking** and no vaping

### Weeks 1–4

- **Gradually increase activity** as comfort allows
- **Contact sports** should be avoided until your surgeon clears you
- **Return to work** depends on the procedure and your job demands — desk work may be possible within a few days, while physically demanding roles may need more recovery time

## Signs that warrant a call to your surgeon

Contact your surgeon if you experience:

- **Fever** above 38°C (100.4°F)
- **Increasing pain** after day 3 (may indicate dry socket or infection)
- **Uncontrolled swelling** that worsens rather than improves after day 4
- **Pus or unusual discharge** from the surgical site
- **Difficulty breathing** or swallowing
- **Numbness** that persists beyond the first 24 hours (though some temporary numbness is common with lower wisdom tooth removal)

## The recovery timeline at a glance

| Timeframe | Typical Experience |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Days 1–3** | Swelling peaks at day 2; manage with ice; liquid/very soft diet |
| **Days 4–7** | Swelling subsides; transition to soft foods; mild discomfort |
| **Week 2** | Most swelling gone; can eat most foods comfortably; stitches removed if non-resorbable |
| **Week 3–4** | Significant healing; most normal activities resumed; gradual return to all foods |
| **Week 6–8** | Complete bone healing (especially after implant placement or jaw surgery) |

## Key takeaway

The first week is the most critical period for healing. Protect the clot, manage swelling, eat supportive foods, and maintain gentle oral hygiene. Most people feel significantly better by day 7 and can resume normal eating within 2–3 weeks. Following your surgeon’s specific instructions is more important than any general guideline — they know your case best.

> This article is general education, not individual medical advice. Always follow the post-operative instructions provided by your surgical team and contact them if you have concerns about your recovery.
