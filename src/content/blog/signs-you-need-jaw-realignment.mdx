Jaw misalignment (malocclusion) can develop over time or be present from birth. While mild cases may not cause functional problems, certain symptoms can indicate that orthognathic surgery could improve both appearance and function.

## Common signs and symptoms

### Functional issues

- **Difficulty chewing or biting**: Food feels difficult to manage, or you frequently bite your cheek or tongue while eating
- **Speech changes**: Persistent lisping, slurring, or difficulty forming certain sounds
- **Chronic jaw pain or TMJ discomfort**: Recurring pain in the jaw joint, especially when chewing or opening wide
- **Limited mouth opening**: Inability to open your mouth comfortably beyond a certain point
- **Jaw locking**: The jaw gets "stuck" open or closed and requires manual manipulation to release

### Structural and aesthetic changes

- **Asymmetrical facial appearance**: One side of the face looks different from the other
- **Protruding upper or lower jaw**: The upper jaw sticks out significantly, or the lower jaw recedes
- **Open bite**: When the back teeth are together, the front teeth don't touch
- **Excessive wear on teeth**: Teeth show unusual wear patterns from abnormal contact
- **Difficulty closing the lips**: Lips don't meet naturally at rest

### Associated conditions

- **Sleep apnea or breathing difficulties**: Particularly if the jaw structure contributes to airway obstruction
- **Chronic headaches**: Especially tension-type headaches related to jaw muscle strain
- **Neck and shoulder pain**: Postural compensation from jaw misalignment

## The assessment process

If you’re experiencing these symptoms, an evaluation typically involves:

1. **Clinical examination**: Assessment of jaw movement, bite alignment, and facial symmetry
2. **Dental impressions**: Models of your teeth to study the occlusion (bite)
3. **Radiographs and CT scans**: 3D imaging of the jaw structure, tooth positions, and facial skeleton
4. **Treatment planning software**: Digital simulation of how jaw repositioning would affect both function and appearance
5. **Multi-disciplinary review**: Collaboration between your oral surgeon, orthodontist, and sometimes a periodontist or prosthodontist

## Is surgery always the answer?

Not necessarily. The treatment approach depends on the severity and type of misalignment:

- **Orthodontics alone**: Some cases can be corrected with braces or clear aligners alone, especially if the misalignment is dental (tooth-related) rather than skeletal
- **Surgical orthodontics**: A combination of braces before surgery, the surgical procedure itself, and continued orthodontics after surgery — this is the standard approach for significant skeletal discrepancies
- **Observation**: Mild cases that don’t affect function or aesthetics may be monitored without active treatment

The decision is ultimately personal and should be made in consultation with your healthcare team, weighing the potential benefits against the risks of surgery.

> This article is general education, not individual medical advice. If you’re experiencing any of the symptoms described, please consult a qualified oral & maxillofacial surgeon for a personalised assessment.
