The wisdom teeth (third molars) typically emerge between ages 17 and 25. For many people, these teeth cause no issues — but for others, they can lead to pain, infection, or crowding.

## When removal is recommended

Your oral & maxillofacial surgeon may recommend removing wisdom teeth if you experience:

- **Impaction**: The tooth is trapped beneath the gum or jawbone and cannot fully emerge
- **Partial eruption**: A portion of the tooth breaks through the gum, creating a flap that can trap bacteria
- **Crowding**: The emerging tooth pushes against adjacent teeth, affecting alignment
- **Decay or gum disease**: Wisdom teeth are hard to clean, making them prone to cavities and periodontitis
- **Cysts or tumors**: Fluid-filled sacs can develop around the tooth base

## What the procedure involves

Wisdom teeth removal is typically performed under local anaesthesia, sedation, or general anaesthesia, depending on the complexity and your comfort level. The surgeon will:

1. Make an incision in the gum tissue to expose the tooth
2. Remove any bone blocking access to the tooth root
3. Divide the tooth into sections if it’s deeply impacted
4. Remove each section carefully
5. Clean the site and suture the gum

## Recovery: what to expect

### First 24–48 hours

- **Swelling and bruising** are normal and typically peak at day 2
- **Ice packs** applied to the cheek can reduce swelling
- **Bite gently** on the gauze pad placed over the extraction site to control bleeding
- **Avoid rinsing**, spitting, or using straws — the clot forming in the socket is essential for healing

### Days 3–7

- **Swelling gradually subsides**; warm compresses may help after day 3
- **Transition to soft foods**: soups, scrambled eggs, yogurt, mashed potatoes, dal
- **Gentle saltwater rinses** (after the first 24 hours) keep the area clean without disturbing the clot
- **Mild discomfort** can be managed with prescribed or over-the-counter medication

### Weeks 2–4

- **Most swelling and bruising resolve** by week 2
- **Socket continues to heal**; small food particles may settle into the area — rinse gently
- **Stitches** (if non-resorbable) are typically removed after 7–10 days
- **Gradual return to normal eating**, starting with softer solids

## When to contact your surgeon

Seek follow-up care if you experience:

- **Fever** or signs of infection (pus, increasing redness, warmth)
- **Uncontrolled bleeding** that doesn’t respond to pressure
- **Severe pain** that worsens after day 3 (may indicate dry socket)
- **Trismus** (difficulty opening your mouth) that persists
- **Numbness** in the lip, chin, or tongue that lasts beyond the first day

## The key takeaway

Wisdom teeth removal is a common procedure with a predictable recovery. Most people return to normal activities within a week. The most important factor is protecting the blood clot that forms in the socket — dislodging it (dry socket) is the most frequent complication, but it’s also preventable with proper aftercare.

> This article is general education, not individual medical advice. If you’re experiencing pain or discomfort from your wisdom teeth, please arrange an assessment with your surgeon.
